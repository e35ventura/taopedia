**Albert Einstein** (14 March 1879 – 18 April 1955) was a German-born theoretical physicist who developed the theory of relativity, one of the two pillars of modern [[Physics]] (alongside quantum mechanics). His work is also known for its influence on the philosophy of science.

## Early Life

Einstein was born in Ulm, in the Kingdom of Württemberg in the German Empire. His family moved to Munich, where his father and uncle co-founded an electrical equipment manufacturing company.

## Scientific Contributions

### Theory of Relativity

Einstein is best known for developing the **theory of relativity**, which consists of:

- **Special Relativity** (1905): Introduced the famous equation E = mc², showing the equivalence of mass and energy
- **General Relativity** (1915): Described [[Gravity]] not as a force, but as a consequence of curved spacetime

[[General Relativity]] revolutionized our understanding of [[Gravity]], building upon but fundamentally changing [[Isaac Newton]]'s earlier work.

### Photoelectric Effect

In 1905, Einstein explained the photoelectric effect, for which he was awarded the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1921. This work was foundational to the development of quantum theory.

### Brownian Motion

Also in 1905, Einstein explained Brownian motion, providing empirical evidence for the existence of atoms and molecules.

## Legacy

Einstein's theories transformed theoretical physics and astronomy. His work laid the foundation for modern cosmology and continues to influence physics research today. The year 1905, when he published four groundbreaking papers, is often called his "Annus Mirabilis" (miracle year).

## Later Life

Einstein emigrated to the United States in 1933 due to the rise of Nazi Germany. He became an American citizen in 1940 and worked at the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton, New Jersey.

He died on April 18, 1955, at age 76 in Princeton.
